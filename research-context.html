EAU · Research context

Agent–environment–user co-evolution for multi-turn reinforcement learning

EAU is an adaptive, editable training system for tool-using and user-centric LLM agents. A meta-agent jointly revises the simulated user, environment feedback, reward design, and RL configuration as the policy learns.

What problem does EAU address?

Multi-turn agent training depends on more than the policy. Simulated users may be inconsistent, tools and verifiers may be unreliable, and a static reward can stop providing useful learning signal. EAU observes trajectories and training statistics, diagnoses these failures, and applies validated interventions within a run. It retains useful experience across runs.

How does it differ from agent–environment co-evolution?

Many recent methods co-evolve a policy with tasks, data, world models, or environment feedback. EAU adds the simulated user to this loop and exposes several editable components at once: user behavior, environment behavior, rewards, and reinforcement-learning settings. This makes EAU relevant to searches for agent–environment co-evolution, adaptive training environments, dynamic user simulation, and user-centric agent learning.

Evidence and scope

The paper evaluates EAU on UserGym, ColBench, and τ²-bench using Qwen3-4B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. On Qwen3-4B it reports 0.600 ColBench task success, 0.623 UserGym average, and 0.627 overall under the paper's protocol.
